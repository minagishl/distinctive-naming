# Sound symbolism

A name's sound shapes its impression before its meaning lands. These are tendencies from research, not rules. Use them to check that the sound does not fight the brief's personality.

## Findings

| Effect | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Back vowels (o, u, a) suggest large, heavy, thick, dark. Front vowels (i, e) suggest small, light, fast. | Klink (2000), Marketing Letters 11, 5–20. Invented names; e.g. "Dutal" was chosen over "Detal" as the bigger laptop. [K][M] |
| People prefer names whose sound matches the product: front-vowel names for convertibles and knives, back-vowel names for SUVs and hammers. | Lowrey & Shrum (2007), Journal of Consumer Research 34(3), 406–414. [L] |
| A back-vowel ice cream name ("Frosh") was imagined creamier and richer than a front-vowel one ("Frish"). | Yorkston & Menon (2004), "A Sound Idea", Journal of Consumer Research, doi:10.1086/383422 (citation confirmed via Crossref; the experiment as reported by NPR; the full text was not reachable). [Y][N] |
| For Japanese speakers, more voiced obstruents (b, d, g, z) and more morae predict larger, heavier, stronger. | Kawahara, Noto & Kumagai (2018), Phonetica: Pokémon names, ρ ≈ 0.25–0.38. [P] |
| Lexicon treats B as a reliable sound across languages (BlackBerry). | Practitioner claim, not a controlled study. [Q] |
| Round-sounding nonwords (bouba) match round shapes and spiky-sounding ones (kiki) match spiky shapes across 25 languages and 10 writing systems, largely independent of script. | Ćwiek et al. (2022), Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B, doi:10.1098/rstb.2020.0390. Useful when pairing a name with a logo shape, or naming for several markets. [B] |
| Easy-to-pronounce names are processed more fluently; IPOs with pronounceable names did better in the days after listing. | Alter & Oppenheimer (2006), PNAS. A short-term effect, not long-term. [A] |

## Applying them

| Personality | Lean toward |
|---|---|
| Light, quick, nimble | Front vowels, voiceless consonants, short |
| Solid, weighty, robust | Back vowels, voiced obstruents, somewhat longer |
| Precise, sharp | Stops (k, t, p) with front vowels |
| Soft, friendly, rounded | Rounded back vowels (o, u) with m and b sounds, as in bouba |

The "precise" and "soft" rows extend the vowel findings and the bouba/kiki correspondence above. They are applications, not separately tested claims.

## Readability checks

- Does it have exactly one katakana reading? The l/r and v/b contrasts and "th" disappear in Japanese.
- Can an English speaker spell it after hearing it? Watch romaji variants (ou / o / oh).
- Say it aloud. Avoid clusters of four or more consonants.
